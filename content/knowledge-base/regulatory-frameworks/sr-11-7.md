## Overview

SR 11-7, issued by the Federal Reserve in April 2011, establishes supervisory guidance on model risk management for banking organizations. It defines model risk as the potential for adverse consequences from decisions based on incorrect or misused model outputs and reports.

The guidance emphasizes that model risk can lead to financial loss, poor strategic decisions, and damage to a bank's reputation. It applies to all models used by banking organizations, including those for credit risk, market risk, operational risk, and Asset-Liability Management (ALM).

## Key Requirements

### Model Risk Management Framework

Banks must establish a comprehensive model risk management framework with clear governance, policies, and controls. This includes:

- **Three Lines of Defense**: Model development, model validation, and board/senior management oversight
- **Model Inventory**: Comprehensive tracking of all models across the organization
- **Model Risk Rating**: Tiering models based on complexity, materiality, and usage
- **Documentation Standards**: Complete documentation of model design, assumptions, and limitations

### Model Development

Effective model development requires rigorous design and testing:

- Sound conceptual framework aligned with intended purpose
- Careful selection of input data and assumptions
- Comprehensive testing and sensitivity analysis
- Documentation of model limitations and appropriate use cases
- Regular review and update cycles

### Independent Model Validation

Model validation must be conducted by qualified staff independent of model development:

- Evaluation of conceptual soundness
- Ongoing monitoring of model performance
- Analysis of model inputs, assumptions, and outputs
- Benchmarking against alternative models
- Outcomes analysis comparing predictions to actual results

## Practical Implications for AI Models

While SR 11-7 predates modern AI/ML systems, its principles remain highly relevant:

**Model Risk Identification**: AI models introduce new forms of model risk including bias, explainability challenges, and adversarial vulnerabilities. Banks must extend SR 11-7's risk identification framework to address these AI-specific risks.

**Validation Challenges**: Traditional validation techniques may be insufficient for complex AI models. Organizations need new validation approaches including:

- Fairness testing across protected classes
- Explainability assessment using SHAP values or similar techniques
- Adversarial robustness testing
- Drift monitoring for data and concept drift

**Documentation Standards**: AI models require enhanced documentation covering:

- Training data lineage and quality assessment
- Hyperparameter tuning methodology
- Model architecture and algorithm selection rationale
- Bias testing results and mitigation strategies

**Governance Adaptation**: SR 11-7's three-line model remains valid but requires AI-specific governance elements:

- AI ethics committees for high-risk use cases
- Data quality and lineage tracking systems
- Continuous monitoring infrastructure
- Regular retraining and validation cycles

## Industry Adoption

Since its issuance, SR 11-7 has become the de facto standard for model risk management in US banking. Most large banks have established dedicated Model Risk Management (MRM) teams responsible for implementing SR 11-7 requirements.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) issued complementary guidance (OCC 2011-12) with similar principles, creating industry-wide alignment on model risk management expectations.

## Evolving Interpretation

Regulatory expectations under SR 11-7 continue to evolve, particularly regarding AI/ML models:

- Examiners increasingly expect banks to apply SR 11-7 rigor to AI models
- Recent supervisory feedback emphasizes continuous monitoring over point-in-time validation
- Focus on third-party model risk for vendor-provided AI solutions
- Emerging expectations for governance of Generative AI applications

Banks implementing AI must recognize that SR 11-7 compliance is table stakes—the real challenge is adapting its principles to the unique characteristics of AI models while maintaining the spirit of rigorous model risk management.
