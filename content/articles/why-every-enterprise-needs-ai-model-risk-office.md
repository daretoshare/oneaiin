## The Governance Gap

Every major financial institution runs hundreds — sometimes thousands — of AI and ML models in production. Credit scoring, fraud detection, anti-money laundering, stress testing, customer lifetime value, dynamic pricing. These models make decisions that affect billions of dollars and millions of customers.

Yet most organizations lack a dedicated function responsible for ensuring these models do what they claim, fail gracefully when they don't, and evolve safely as the world changes around them.

This is the governance gap. And it's growing wider as GenAI and agentic AI systems enter the enterprise.

## What SR 11-7 Taught Us

The Federal Reserve's SR 11-7 guidance on model risk management, issued in 2011, established the gold standard for how financial institutions should govern models. Its three pillars — model development, model validation, and model usage — created a framework that has stood the test of time.

But SR 11-7 was written for traditional statistical models. The AI models we deploy today are fundamentally different in their complexity, opacity, and rate of change. An enterprise AI Model Risk Office must extend SR 11-7's principles to cover:

- **Foundation models and LLMs** that can't be validated with traditional backtesting
- **Agentic AI systems** that make autonomous decisions in sequence
- **Continuous learning models** that evolve without explicit retraining
- **Multi-model pipelines** where risk compounds across components

## The Three Functions of a Model Risk Office

A well-designed AI Model Risk Office serves three critical functions:

### 1. Independent Validation

Every model that touches a business decision needs independent review — not by the team that built it, but by a qualified, separate function. This includes conceptual soundness review, data quality assessment, performance benchmarking, and ongoing monitoring.

### 2. Governance and Standards

The MRO sets and enforces standards for model documentation, approval workflows, change management, and retirement. It maintains the model inventory and ensures every model has an accountable owner.

### 3. Innovation Enablement

This is the function most organizations miss. A good MRO doesn't just say "no" — it provides safe pathways for experimentation. It pre-approves sandbox environments, defines acceptable risk thresholds for pilot programs, and creates fast-track validation for low-risk models.

## Beyond Banking

While SR 11-7 is a banking regulation, its principles apply everywhere AI models drive decisions. Healthcare organizations deploying clinical decision support, pharmaceutical companies using AI for drug discovery, insurance companies automating underwriting — all face the same fundamental challenge: how do you trust a model you can't fully explain?

The AI Model Risk Office is the answer. Not as a bureaucratic overhead, but as the organizational function that makes enterprise AI adoption sustainable, trustworthy, and scalable.
